# Release-ready: from Suno take to a song you can publish

How to turn a Suno generation into a release that sounds professional and holds up on Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music and other platforms. The approach is to **improve the record and own your part of it**, not to hide how it was made.

## What this skill will not do

It does not help remove watermarks or audio fingerprints, strip or forge metadata, or otherwise disguise a track's AI origin, and it does not help answer "no" on a distributor's AI-usage question when AI was used. When asked, say so in one sentence and offer the workflow below.

Why this matters for the user, not only as a rule:
- Distributors and streaming services ask about AI use and are adding AI labels and credits. Policies change often, so check the current terms of your distributor and of each platform before every release.
- A misdeclared track risks takedown, withheld royalties or a closed distributor account, which is far worse than an "AI-assisted" credit.
- Suno's own terms set what you may do with a generation; commercial release generally requires a paid plan at the time the song was generated. Check Suno's current terms.

## 1. Generate for release quality

Add to STYLE when the take is meant for release:
`Clean transients, natural cymbal decay, intelligible lyrics, dry upfront lead vocal, uncluttered arrangement.`

Add to EXCLUDE (pick what fits the budget):
`lo-fi artifacts, metallic shimmer, phasey vocals, warbly pitch, smeared cymbals, muddy low end`

Then:
- Generate several takes and pick on the chorus, the vocal and the low end, not on the intro.
- Use Suno's editing tools (replace section, extend, cover) to fix one bad section instead of regenerating the whole song.

## 2. Find and fix what sounds generated

Listen on headphones and on a phone speaker. Typical problems and fixes:

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Mispronounced or mumbled words | Replace the section in Suno, or re-record the line |
| Metallic, swirly hi-hats and cymbals | De-ess/EQ the stem, or replace with programmed or real drums |
| Vocal sounds phasey or chorused | Re-record the vocal, or use the dry vocal stem with fresh processing |
| Smeared transients, weak kick/snare | Drum replacement or layered samples on the drum stem |
| Muddy or buried low end | Re-record or reprogram the bass; high-pass the other stems |
| Lyrics drift from what you wrote | Fix the lyric sheet, then replace the section |
| Abrupt or chopped ending | Edit the ending in the DAW, or extend in Suno and crossfade |

## 3. Add real human work (this is what makes it yours)

The more you perform, write and decide, the stronger your authorship and the better the record:
1. **Export stems** (vocals, drums, bass, other) where your plan allows, and import them into a DAW.
2. **Re-record the lead vocal** with a real singer, keeping the Suno melody as a guide. This is the single biggest upgrade.
3. **Re-record or re-amp guitars and bass**; replace or layer drums.
4. **Change the arrangement**: cut, extend, add a real intro, a new bridge or harmonies.
5. **Mix and master** by ear (or with an engineer) to a streaming loudness target, comparing with commercial reference tracks.

Keep the project files, stems, lyric drafts and session takes: they document your creative contribution.

## 4. Credit and declare honestly

- Answer the distributor's AI question truthfully and describe what was AI and what was human.
- Credits example: `Lyrics: <name>. Composition: <name> with Suno (AI). Lead vocal recorded by <name>. Mixed and mastered by <name>.`
- Copyright protection generally covers the human-authored parts (your lyrics, your recordings, your arrangement choices), and rules vary by country. For anything high-stakes, ask a music lawyer.

## Copy-paste prompt: release plan

Use this in any AI assistant after the song is generated:

```text
I generated this song with Suno and want to release it on streaming platforms, honestly declared as AI-assisted.
Song: <TITLE>. Genre: <GENRE>. What I have: <full mix / stems>. What I can record myself: <vocals / guitar / nothing>.

Give me:
1. A listening checklist of likely generation artifacts for this genre and how to fix each one (Suno edit, DAW fix or re-record).
2. A human-production plan ranked by impact: what to re-record or replace first.
3. Mix and master notes with two commercial reference tracks of the same style.
4. A short, honest credits line and an "AI usage" description I can paste into my distributor's form.
Do not suggest removing watermarks, altering metadata or hiding AI use.
```
